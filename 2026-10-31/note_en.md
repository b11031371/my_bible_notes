## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** Song of Solomon 8:1-14

**1** Oh, that you were like my brother, Who nursed at my mother's breasts! If I should find you outside, I would kiss you; I would not be despised.

**2** I would lead you and bring you Into the house of my mother, She who used to instruct me. I would cause you to drink of spiced wine, Of the juice of my pomegranate.

**3** His left hand is under my head, And his right hand embraces me.

**4** I charge you, O daughters of Jerusalem, Do not stir up nor awaken love Until it pleases.

**5** Who is this coming up from the wilderness, Leaning upon her beloved? I awakened you under the apple tree. There your mother brought you forth; There she who bore you brought you forth.

**6** Set me as a seal upon your heart, As a seal upon your arm; For love is as strong as death, Jealousy as cruel as the grave; Its flames are flames of fire, A most vehement flame.

**7** Many waters cannot quench love, Nor can the floods drown it. If a man would give for love All the wealth of his house, It would be utterly despised.

**8** We have a little sister, And she has no breasts. What shall we do for our sister In the day when she is spoken for?

**9** If she is a wall, We will build upon her A battlement of silver; And if she is a door, We will enclose her With boards of cedar.

**10** I am a wall, And my breasts like towers; Then I became in his eyes As one who found peace.

**11** Solomon had a vineyard at Baal Hamon; He leased the vineyard to keepers; Everyone was to bring for its fruit A thousand silver coins.

**12** My own vineyard is before me. You, O Solomon, may have a thousand, And those who tend its fruit two hundred.

**13** You who dwell in the gardens, The companions listen for your voice— Let me hear it!

**14** Make haste, my beloved, And be like a gazelle Or a young stag On the mountains of spices.

## II. Key Summary

- She wishes her beloved were like a brother who nursed at her mother's breasts, so that she could kiss him outside and not be despised; she would bring him into her mother's house. Then she charges the daughters of Jerusalem again: "Do not stir up nor awaken love Until it pleases" (vv. 1-4).
- Someone asks, "Who is this coming up from the wilderness, Leaning upon her beloved?" She asks him to set her as a seal upon his heart and arm, saying love is as strong as death and many waters cannot quench it; if a man gave all the wealth of his house for love, it would be utterly despised (vv. 5-7).
- The brothers talk about what to do for their little sister, "If she is a wall… if she is a door"; she says, "I am a wall," and she became in his eyes as one who found peace. Solomon's vineyard brings a thousand silver coins, and she says, "My own vineyard is before me." Finally the beloved says, "Let me hear it!" and she answers, "Make haste, my beloved" (vv. 8-14).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### Bringing Her Beloved Back to Where It Began

> She happily takes the image of the wall for herself… she is announcing her maturity, and her readiness for the love and marriage she has been celebrating. [1]

At the start of the book, she was the girl her brothers sent to keep the vineyards (1:6). In this chapter she comes back home, and when her brothers say, "If she is a wall… if she is a door," she takes up the word "wall" and says, "I am a wall, And my breasts like towers" (vv. 9-10).

The words her brothers used out of worry, she now uses to announce that she has grown up.

---

### Mature, and Still Saying "Make Haste"

> In every way we have seen a marriage in maturity. … in the greater security of the wife, in her playful freedom to initiate love, and finally in the fullness of their relationship the poet has sketched a revealing portrait of the model couple. [2]

You can see these things in this chapter. She takes the lead: "I would lead you and bring you Into the house of my mother" (v. 2), unlike 1:4, when "The king has brought me into his chambers." She says, "I am a wall," and she became in his eyes "As one who found peace" (v. 10). In 2:17 she said, "Until the day breaks And the shadows flee away, Turn, my beloved," but the last line of the book is "Make haste, my beloved" (v. 14): the relationship has matured, yet it has not lost its passion and excitement [3].

Mature love is more secure and freer to reach out; from "until the day breaks" to "make haste," her longing to see him has not faded at all.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

In its last chapter, the Song says, "Many waters cannot quench love, Nor can the floods drown it." Do you long to have, and to keep for good, a love this passionate and sweet?

From "Do not stir up nor awaken love Until it pleases" (2:7), to seeking him at night but not finding him (3:1-2), to opening the door only to find that her beloved "had turned away and was gone" (5:6), this love has known both waiting and missing each other. In the last chapter she comes home leaning closely on her beloved (8:5) and asks him to set her as a seal upon his heart, like pressing a seal, so that they would belong to each other for as long as they live (8:6) [4]; she tells the brothers who once worried about her, "I am a wall," announcing that she has grown up (8:10); and she says, "My own vineyard is before me": she herself is hers alone to decide about (8:12) [1]. All along the way, it comes down to what she says: "Many waters cannot quench love, Nor can the floods drown it" (8:7).
