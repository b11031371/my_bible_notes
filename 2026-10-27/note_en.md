## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** Song of Solomon 4:1-16

**1** Behold, you are fair, my love! Behold, you are fair! You have dove’s eyes behind your veil. Your hair is like a flock of goats, Going down from Mount Gilead.

**2** Your teeth are like a flock of shorn sheep Which have come up from the washing, Every one of which bears twins, And none is barren among them.

**3** Your lips are like a strand of scarlet, And your mouth is lovely. Your temples behind your veil Are like a piece of pomegranate.

**4** Your neck is like the tower of David, Built for an armory, On which hang a thousand bucklers, All shields of mighty men.

**5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, Twins of a gazelle, Which feed among the lilies.

**6** Until the day breaks And the shadows flee away, I will go my way to the mountain of myrrh And to the hill of frankincense.

**7** You are all fair, my love, And there is no spot in you.

**8** Come with me from Lebanon, my spouse, With me from Lebanon. Look from the top of Amana, From the top of Senir and Hermon, From the lions’ dens, From the mountains of the leopards.

**9** You have ravished my heart, My sister, my spouse; You have ravished my heart With one look of your eyes, With one link of your necklace.

**10** How fair is your love, My sister, my spouse! How much better than wine is your love, And the scent of your perfumes Than all spices!

**11** Your lips, O my spouse, Drip as the honeycomb; Honey and milk are under your tongue; And the fragrance of your garments Is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

**12** A garden enclosed Is my sister, my spouse, A spring shut up, A fountain sealed.

**13** Your plants are an orchard of pomegranates With pleasant fruits, Fragrant henna with spikenard,

**14** Spikenard and saffron, Calamus and cinnamon, With all trees of frankincense, Myrrh and aloes, With all the chief spices—

**15** A fountain of gardens, A well of living waters, And streams from Lebanon.

**16** Awake, O north wind, And come, O south! Blow upon my garden, That its spices may flow out. Let my beloved come to his garden And eat its pleasant fruits.

## II. Key Summary

- He praises her eyes, hair, teeth, lips, temples, neck, and breasts, comparing them to dove's eyes, a flock of goats, shorn sheep, a strand of scarlet, a piece of pomegranate, the tower of David, and two fawns; then he says, "You are all fair, my love, and there is no spot in you." (vv. 1–7)
- He calls her "my spouse" and asks her to come with him from Lebanon, to look from the top of Amana, Senir and Hermon, from the lions' dens and the mountains of the leopards; he calls her "my sister, my spouse," says she has ravished his heart with one look of her eyes, and says her love is much better than wine. (vv. 8–11)
- He calls her "a garden enclosed… a spring shut up, a fountain sealed," with pomegranates, spikenard, saffron, myrrh, aloes, and all the chief spices, and "a fountain of gardens, a well of living waters"; then she says, "Awake, O north wind, and come, O south!" and "Let my beloved come to his garden and eat its pleasant fruits." (vv. 12–16)

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "You Are All Fair"

> "After giving a seven-fold description of his maiden's beauty, the beloved summarizes his observations. She was more than fair; she was all fair, and there was no spot in her." [1]

In v. 1 he says, "Behold, you are fair! Behold, you are fair!" Then he goes from her eyes, hair, teeth, and lips all the way to her neck and breasts (vv. 1–5). After all that, in v. 7 his words become "all fair… there is no spot in you."

He looked at her one part at a time, and his conclusion was "all."

---

### "A Garden Enclosed"

> "'Enclosed,' 'shut up,' 'sealed': these refer to the bride's chastity." [2]

Verse 12 uses three pictures in a row: a garden enclosed, a spring shut up, a fountain sealed. In v. 16 she first says, "Blow upon my garden," and then, "Let my beloved come to his garden."

The garden that was enclosed is opened when she herself asks her beloved to come in.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Think of someone you care about. What about them looks fair to you? What would you do to let them know?

The bridegroom, facing his bride, goes from her eyes and hair all the way to her neck, naming her beauty one part at a time (vv. 1–5); when he finishes, he says, "You are all fair, my love, and there is no spot in you" (v. 7).
