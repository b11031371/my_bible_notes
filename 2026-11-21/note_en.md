## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 1:8-14

**8** For we do not want you to be ignorant, brethren, of our trouble which came to us in Asia: that we were burdened beyond measure, above strength, so that we despaired even of life.

**9** Yes, we had the sentence of death in ourselves, that we should not trust in ourselves but in God who raises the dead,

**10** who delivered us from so great a death, and does deliver us; in whom we trust that He will still deliver us,

**11** you also helping together in prayer for us, that thanks may be given by many persons on our behalf for the gift granted to us through many.

**12** For our boasting is this: the testimony of our conscience that we conducted ourselves in the world in simplicity and godly sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom but by the grace of God, and more abundantly toward you.

**13** For we are not writing any other things to you than what you read or understand. Now I trust you will understand, even to the end

**14** (as also you have understood us in part), that we are your boast as you also are ours, in the day of the Lord Jesus.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul says that the trouble which came to them in Asia burdened them beyond measure, above strength, so that they despaired even of life and had the sentence of death in themselves — so that they should not trust in themselves but in God who raises the dead (vv. 8–9).
- Paul says God delivered them from so great a death, does deliver them, and will still deliver them; he asks the Corinthians to help together in prayer, so that many would give thanks for the gift granted to them (vv. 10–11).
- Paul says their boasting is the testimony of their conscience: they conducted themselves in the world in simplicity and godly sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom but by the grace of God. He writes nothing other than what the Corinthians read or understand, and hopes they will be each other's boast in the day of the Lord Jesus (vv. 12–14).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "That we should not trust in ourselves but in God who raises the dead"

> God often brings his people into great straits, that they may apprehend their own insufficiency to help themselves, and may be induced to place their trust and hope in his all-sufficiency. [1]

Paul piles up "burdened beyond measure," "above strength," and "despaired even of life" to describe his time in Asia, until he had the sentence of death in himself (vv. 8–9). He says this was "that we should not trust in ourselves" but in God who raises the dead — and God's deliverance comes in three tenses — He delivered, does deliver, and will still deliver (v. 10) [2].

When Paul came to the point of not expecting to survive, he relied on the God who raises the dead.

---

### "Not with fleshly wisdom but by the grace of God"

> Some in the Corinthian church, because of his recent conduct — especially the change in his plan to visit Corinth — accused him of saying yes and no at once. Paul says his conscience can testify to his holiness and sincerity toward people, especially toward the Corinthian believers. [3]

The word in verse 12 can be translated "purity"; its root meaning is something held up in the sunlight, with no false color, nothing mixed in or hidden [3]. So in verse 13 Paul says he writes "nothing other than what you read or understand" — his letter has no aim beyond its plain meaning [3].

When people doubted whether he kept his word, Paul pointed to a conscience laid out in the sunlight.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Think of something that was once so big you felt you truly couldn't get through it on your own. When you realized your strength and your ideas had run out, what did you do then?

In Asia Paul was burdened beyond measure, above strength, and despaired even of life; he says this was "that we should not trust in ourselves but in God who raises the dead" (vv. 8–9), and he could also say, "in whom we trust that He will still deliver us" (v. 10).
