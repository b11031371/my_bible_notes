## II. Key Summary

- The guide first brought Ezekiel into the outer court and measured the north and south gateways; they matched the east gate exactly—fifty cubits long and twenty-five cubits wide—and seven steps led up to each of them.
- They then went through the south, east, and north gateways of the inner court, all built to the same measurements, except that eight steps led up to these; a chamber by the gateposts was where the burnt offering was washed, and eight tables in the vestibule were used for slaughtering the sacrifices.
- The inner court held chambers for the singers and chambers for the priests who had charge of the temple and of the altar (the sons of Zadok, from the sons of Levi); the court measured one hundred cubits by one hundred cubits, foursquare, with the altar in front of the temple.

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### The Farther In, The Higher Up

> The ascent into the outer court at each gate was by seven steps, but the ascent into the inner court at each gate was by eight steps. This is expressly taken notice of (v. 31, 34, 37), to signify that the nearer we approach to God the more we should rise above this world and the things of it. [1]

The whole passage keeps repeating the same measurements; the one thing that changes is the number of steps—seven to the outer court, eight to the inner. Getting close to God happens one step at a time, and each step in means loosening your grip on something out here.

---

### Already Set Apart, Still Washed Again

> There also they washed the burnt-offerings (v. 38), to intimate that before we draw near to God's altar we must have every thing in readiness, must wash our hands, our hearts, those spiritual sacrifices, and so compass God's altar. [2]

The animal had already been picked out and set apart for God, and it still got washed one more time at the gate. Standing before God means stopping first to get yourself ready, not just rushing in.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

If getting close to God works the way this passage describes—one step up at a time—which step do you think you're standing on right now?

Seven steps led up to the outer court, eight to the inner, and the ground kept rising the farther in you went. God isn't asking you to jump straight to the top; He's inviting you up one more step.
