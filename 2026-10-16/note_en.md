**21** The sons of Laadan, the descendants of the Gershonites of Laadan, heads of their fathers’ houses, of Laadan the Gershonite: Jehieli.

**22** The sons of Jehieli, Zetham and Joel his brother, were over the treasuries of the house of the Lord.

**24** Shebuel the son of Gershom, the son of Moses, was overseer of the treasuries.

## II. Key Summary

- Of the Levites, Ahijah was over the treasuries of the house of God and the treasuries of the dedicated things; Zetham and Joel, sons of Jehieli the Gershonite, were over the treasuries of the house of the Lord; Shebuel, the grandson of Moses, was overseer of the treasuries.
- Shelomith and his brethren were over the treasuries of the dedicated things: spoils won in battles that King David, the heads of fathers' houses, and the captains of the army had dedicated to maintain the house of the Lord. Everything Samuel the seer, Saul, Abner, and Joab had dedicated was also under their hand.
- Chenaniah and his sons served as officials and judges over Israel outside Jerusalem; Hashabiah and 1,700 able men oversaw Israel west of the Jordan, and Jerijah's 2,700 brethren served over the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh, "for every matter pertaining to God and the affairs of the king." These capable men were found at Jazer of Gilead in the fortieth year of David's reign.

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### Spoils of War, Given to the Lord's House

> When Israel's army went to war, they never relied on their own name; they fought relying solely on the name of the Lord. Therefore the ultimate victor was only the Lord God, not the captains of thousands, the captains of hundreds, or the king. So all the spoils belonged to the Lord. [1]

King David and the captains over thousands and hundreds and of the army dedicated spoils won in battles to maintain the house of the Lord (vv. 26–27), and what Samuel, Saul, Abner, and Joab had dedicated was placed under Shelomith's hand too (v. 28). They fought the battles, and what they won went to the Lord's house.

---

### Work Outside the Temple Is Still the Lord's Business

> Even outside the temple, they were still handling "the business of the Lord and of the king," and all of it needed to be set apart as holy and done with a heart of worship. [2]

Chenaniah and his sons served over Israel outside Jerusalem (v. 29), and Hashabiah's and Jerijah's brethren served on both sides of the Jordan (vv. 30, 32). They worked outside the temple, and Scripture calls their work "the business of the Lord."

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Chenaniah and his sons served as officials and judges "outside Jerusalem," away from the temple. Pick one thing you will do outside church tomorrow. How will you do it?

Chenaniah and his sons served Israel outside Jerusalem, and King David made Jerijah's brethren officials over the tribes east of the Jordan (vv. 29, 32); the ability and the chance to serve are free gifts (1 Cor 4:7), and what they did outside the temple Scripture calls "every matter pertaining to God and the affairs of the king" [2].
