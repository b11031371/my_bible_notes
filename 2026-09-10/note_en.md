## II. Key Summary

- The psalmist declares that the LORD is righteous, His judgments upright, and His testimonies righteous and very faithful; His word is very pure, His law is truth, and His righteousness is everlasting (vv. 137-142, 144).
- The psalmist says he is small and despised and that trouble and anguish have overtaken him, yet he does not forget God's precepts and calls God's commandments his delights; he is also consumed with zeal because his enemies have forgotten God's words (vv. 139, 141, 143).
- The psalmist cries out for salvation with his whole heart, rising before dawn and staying awake through the night watches to meditate on God's word; he notes that those who follow wickedness draw near, while the LORD is near and His testimonies are founded forever (vv. 145-152).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### When you are looked down on, you need God's words even more

> When we are small and despised we have the more need to remember God's precepts, that we may have them to support us under the pressures of a low condition. [1]

The psalmist does not say, "I'm impressive, so I can handle this." He says, "I am small and despised, yet I do not forget Your precepts." What holds him up is not how people rate him, but the fact that what God said has not changed.

---

### They draw near, but You are near

> Note the realism of the double statement, They draw near…but thou art near. The threat is not glossed over; it is put in perspective by a bigger fact. [2]

The psalmist does not pretend the pressure isn't real. He holds two things together: the trouble really is closing in, and God is closer still. Faith isn't shrinking the problem; it's knowing Someone is nearer than it is.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

What is closing in on you lately that makes you feel like you can't hold on much longer?

The psalmist didn't pretend it wasn't there — he just kept saying one other thing at the same time: "You are near, O LORD."
