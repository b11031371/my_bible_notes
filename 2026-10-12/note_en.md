## II. Key Summary

- When David was old, he made Solomon king and gathered the leaders, priests, and Levites; the 38,000 Levites aged thirty and above were divided into four groups: temple workers, officers and judges, gatekeepers, and musicians who praised the Lord (vv. 1–5).
- The chapter lists the family heads of the Levites under Gershon, Kohath, and Merari; Aaron and his sons were set apart to burn incense, minister to God, and bless in His name, while the sons of Moses were counted with the rest of the tribe of Levi (vv. 6–23).
- David said God had given His people rest in Jerusalem, so the Levites no longer had to carry the tabernacle; Levites from twenty years old were now counted, and their duties became helping the priests, purifying holy things, preparing the showbread and grain offerings, keeping the measures, and standing every morning and evening to thank and praise the Lord (vv. 24–32).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### No More Carrying the Tent: Becoming the Priests' "Hands"

> "Serve" literally means "hand" — it means helping the priests do their work, becoming the priests' "hands." … In this passage David rearranged the Levites' work so that they could truly become "the hands of the priests." Are we willing and able to be "the hands of our church leaders," following their lead and doing a whole lot of service that looks tedious and lowly, yet matters? [1]

Once the tent no longer had to be moved, the Levites went from hauling heavy loads to washing vessels, baking bread, and checking measures beside the priests. Hardly anyone saw this work, but the temple couldn't run without those "hands."

---

### Every Morning and Evening, Standing to Say Thanks

> The Chronicler mentioned many specific duties of the Levites (purifying all holy things…with the showbread…what is baked in the pan). Yet he included among them this most important duty: to stand every morning to thank and praise the LORD. This was essential among the duties of the Levites and the priests, and could never be neglected. [2]

Right in the middle of a long list of washing and baking chores sits "stand morning and evening to give thanks and praise." No matter how busy or ordinary the day gets, thanking God belongs on the daily schedule and shouldn't get squeezed out.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

This week, whose "hands" were you — what's one small thing you did to help them?

In verse 28, the word for "serve" literally means "hand." As the priests' hands, the Levites washed vessels, baked bread, and kept the measures. These chores looked small, yet the passage calls them "the work of the service of the house of God."
