## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 4:1-10

**1** Therefore, since we have this ministry, as we have received mercy, we do not lose heart.

**2** But we have renounced the hidden things of shame, not walking in craftiness nor handling the word of God deceitfully, but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God.

**3** But even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled to those who are perishing,

**4** whose minds the god of this age has blinded, who do not believe, lest the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God, should shine on them.

**5** For we do not preach ourselves, but Christ Jesus the Lord, and ourselves your bondservants for Jesus' sake.

**6** For it is the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness, who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.

**7** But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellence of the power may be of God and not of us.

**8** We are hard-pressed on every side, yet not crushed; we are perplexed, but not in despair;

**9** persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed—

**10** always carrying about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus, that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our body.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul says that since they have this ministry, as they have received mercy, they do not lose heart; they have renounced the hidden things of shame, not walking in craftiness nor handling the word of God deceitfully, but by manifestation of the truth commending themselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God (vv. 1–2).
- Paul says that if their gospel is veiled, it is veiled to those who are perishing, whose minds the god of this age has blinded; they do not preach themselves but Christ Jesus the Lord, for the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness has shone in their hearts (vv. 3–6).
- Paul says they have this treasure in earthen vessels, so that the excellence of the power may be of God; they are hard-pressed on every side yet not crushed, struck down but not destroyed, always carrying about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus, that the life of Jesus may also be manifested in their body (vv. 7–10).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "Commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God"

> "Commending ourselves to every man's conscience": notice that Paul does not say he commends himself to every man, but "to every man's conscience." That is to say, even if people oppose us in their feelings, or outwardly reject us out of fear of evil powers, in their conscience they still accept us. [1]

The word translated "deceitfully" is found only here in the New Testament, and means "to dilute or adulterate"; Paul did not mix human ingenuity into the message or water it down to accommodate his audience [2]. This ties back to the dispute earlier in the letter: some came with letters of commendation (3:1), and many were peddling the word of God (2:17).

Paul's "letter of commendation" is the truth set out plainly, left to every person's conscience to judge in the sight of God.

---

### "We have this treasure in earthen vessels"

> This passage repeatedly stresses the uselessness and fragility of the vessel, the weakness and lowliness of the apostles, to prove that the great power of God's gospel is not affected by human weakness and is carried through all the same. … Though living every day for Christ's sake on the edge of danger and death, by the Spirit who dwells within and gives life and light, this fragile and perishable body can, in this life, experience the power of Jesus' resurrection from the dead. [1]

The earthen vessel is like the clay lamps in the Corinthian market: cheap and easily broken, yet useful as long as they let the light through [1]. "The dying of the Lord Jesus" in verse 10 is Paul's constant position, like that of the Lord Jesus, despised and delivered to death; and in that very body, "the life of Jesus" is also manifested [1].

So the four "yet"s of verses 8–9 are the moments when the clay pot is broken and the light shines out from within: what people see is "that the excellence of the power may be of God and not of us" (v. 7) [2].

## IV. Reflection Question

If you were telling a friend about Jesus, and there were parts he or she might not want to hear, how would you say it? What would you want your friend to remember afterward?

Paul did not walk in craftiness nor handle the word of God deceitfully, but set forth the truth plainly (v. 2), because, as he says, "we do not preach ourselves, but Christ Jesus the Lord" (v. 5).
