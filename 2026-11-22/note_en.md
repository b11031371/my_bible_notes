## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 1:15-24

**15** And in this confidence I intended to come to you before, that you might have a second benefit—

**16** to pass by way of you to Macedonia, to come again from Macedonia to you, and be helped by you on my way to Judea.

**17** Therefore, when I was planning this, did I do it lightly? Or the things I plan, do I plan according to the flesh, that with me there should be Yes, Yes, and No, No?

**18** But as God is faithful, our word to you was not Yes and No.

**19** For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was preached among you by us—by me, Silvanus, and Timothy—was not Yes and No, but in Him was Yes.

**20** For all the promises of God in Him are Yes, and in Him Amen, to the glory of God through us.

**24** Not that we have dominion over your faith, but are fellow workers for your joy; for by faith you stand.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul says he intended to come to Corinth so they might have a second benefit: passing by way of them to Macedonia, coming again from Macedonia, and being helped by them on his way to Judea. He asks: when I planned this, did I do it lightly, according to the flesh, so that with me there should be Yes, Yes, and No, No? (vv. 15–17)
- Paul says that as God is faithful, the Son of God, Jesus Christ, preached among them by him, Silvanus, and Timothy, was not Yes and No, but in Him was Yes; all the promises of God in Him are Yes. It is God who establishes and anointed them, who also sealed them and gave the Spirit in their hearts as a guarantee (vv. 18–22).
- Paul calls God as witness against his soul that he came no more to Corinth to spare them; they do not have dominion over the Corinthians' faith, but are fellow workers for their joy, for by faith they stand (vv. 23–24).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "In Him was Yes"

> Paul alludes to an important principle: The message affects the messenger. Paul could not so sincerely and so strongly preach a Jesus who is not Yes and No and be untouched by that Jesus. [1]

In the previous section (1:12-14) Paul spoke of his conscience; here he answers the charge of being fickle head-on. "Amen" in verse 20 can be read as "through Him we can all say Amen" — that is, everyone agrees; if the Corinthians could witness that a matter as weighty as the gospel was true, what reason would Paul have to say yes and no about something as minor as travel plans [2]?

The Christ he preached is only "Yes," so the one who preaches Him will not say yes and no at once.

---

### "To spare you I came no more to Corinth"

> The Corinthian believers stand before God by faith, as a result of receiving God's grace directly; the apostle has no right to demand this of them. Paul's responsibility was to help them find joy, and for this he decided to postpone his visit, so that everyone could be glad when they met. [2]

"I call God as witness against my soul" in verse 23 is a serious oath, and Paul insists it was out of concern for the Corinthian Christians that he did not come [1]. His last visit to Corinth had been a visit "in sorrow" (2:1), so this time he chose to wait rather than make them sorrowful again [2].

When Paul changed his plans, what was on his heart was whether they could meet with joy.

## IV. Reflection Question

If someone asked you, "Does the Jesus you believe in keep His word?", which thing He did would come to your mind?

Some said Paul said yes and no at once. Paul answered, "Our word to you was not Yes and No" (v. 18). For the Jesus Christ he preached "in Him was Yes," and "all the promises of God in Him are Yes" (vv. 19–20).
