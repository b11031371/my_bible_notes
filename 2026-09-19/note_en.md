## II. Key Summary

- Abishai lifted up his spear against three hundred men and killed them, won a name among the three and became their captain, though he did not attain to the first three.

- Benaiah killed two lion-like heroes of Moab, went down and killed a lion in a pit on a snowy day, and wrested an Egyptian's spear from his hand and killed him with it; David appointed him over his guard.

- Verses 26-47 list the mighty warriors name by name, including Naharai the armorbearer of Joab, Zelek the Ammonite, Uriah the Hittite, and Ithmah the Moabite.

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### Someone was standing beside them in all of this

> How could one slay 300 and another the same number, another two lion-like men, and another an Egyptian giant, if they had not had the extraordinary presence of God with them, according to that promise, Jos. 23:10, One man of you shall chase a thousand, for the Lord your God fighteth for you? [1]

The passage gives us very concrete numbers for Abishai and Benaiah: three hundred men, one lion, an Egyptian five cubits tall. But what those numbers are pointing to is God standing beside them, not how strong they were on their own.

---

### Every name on the list counts

> Among these mighty men of David's kingdom there were men from the twelve tribes and also Levites; there were Israelites and also foreigners; there were men who "killed three hundred at one time" and also a man who "bore the armor." Their abilities and their backgrounds were different, yet the Spirit counted them all the same. [2]

From verse 26 on it is just a long run of names, easy to skim past. Yet Scripture writes in even Naharai, "the armorbearer of Joab the son of Zeruiah," and notes exactly what he did; an Ammonite and a Moabite are on the list too.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Today's passage is a list of names. Some killed three hundred men; one "bore the armor for Joab." What is something you did recently that nobody saw and nobody mentioned?

Scripture put that armorbearer on the list and spelled out exactly what he was doing — with God, nothing gets left off.
