## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** Song of Solomon 7:1-13

**1** How beautiful are your feet in sandals, O prince's daughter! The curves of your thighs are like jewels, The work of the hands of a skillful workman.

**2** Your navel is a rounded goblet; It lacks no blended beverage. Your waist is a heap of wheat Set about with lilies.

**3** Your two breasts are like two fawns, Twins of a gazelle.

**4** Your neck is like an ivory tower, Your eyes like the pools in Heshbon By the gate of Bath Rabbim. Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon Which looks toward Damascus.

**5** Your head crowns you like Mount Carmel, And the hair of your head is like purple; A king is held captive by your tresses.

**6** How fair and how pleasant you are, O love, with your delights!

**7** This stature of yours is like a palm tree, And your breasts like its clusters.

**8** I said, "I will go up to the palm tree, I will take hold of its branches." Let now your breasts be like clusters of the vine, The fragrance of your breath like apples,

**9** And the roof of your mouth like the best wine. The wine goes down smoothly for my beloved, Moving gently the lips of sleepers.

**10** I am my beloved's, And his desire is toward me.

**11** Come, my beloved, Let us go forth to the field; Let us lodge in the villages.

**12** Let us get up early to the vineyards; Let us see if the vine has budded, Whether the grape blossoms are open, And the pomegranates are in bloom. There I will give you my love.

**13** The mandrakes give off a fragrance, And at our gates are pleasant fruits, All manner, new and old, Which I have laid up for you, my beloved.

## II. Key Summary

- She is called "O prince's daughter," and the description starts with her feet in sandals, then compares her to jewels, a rounded goblet, a heap of wheat, fawns, an ivory tower, the pools in Heshbon, the tower of Lebanon, and Mount Carmel, ending with "A king is held captive by your tresses" (vv. 1-5).
- Next come the words "How fair and how pleasant you are," her stature is compared to a palm tree, "I will go up to the palm tree, I will take hold of its branches," and the roof of her mouth is like the best wine (vv. 6-9).
- The Shulamite answers, "I am my beloved's, And his desire is toward me," then invites her beloved to go out to the field, lodge in the villages, and get up early to see if the vines have budded, saying, "There I will give you my love," and that the pleasant fruits at their gates, new and old, are what "I have laid up for you" (vv. 9-13).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "Desire" Goes Back to the Beginning

> It is my duty to have my desire towards him as my Husband, according to God's sentence, Genesis 3:16, but it is a wonderful condescension in him, that his desire is towards me. [1]

In the Old Testament, the word "desire" appears only here and in Genesis 3:16 and 4:7 [2]. In Genesis 3:16, after people turned away from God, God said to the woman, "Your desire shall be for your husband, And he shall rule over you." In 7:10 the same word points the other way: "his desire is toward me," right after "I am my beloved's."

In Genesis, desire came tied to "rule"; here, she belongs to him, and his desire is toward her.

---

### From Spring to Spring

> The poet thus reveals that their relationship has gone from spring to spring, that now it has experienced a full cycle of growth. [3]

In 2:10-13 the beloved came to invite her: "Rise up, my love, my fair one, And come away," when "the winter is past" and "the vines with the tender grapes give a good smell." In 6:11 he went down to the garden himself, "To see whether the vine had budded And the pomegranates had bloomed." In 7:11-12 she is the one saying, "Come, my beloved," asking him to get up early and "see if the vine has budded, Whether the grape blossoms are open, And the pomegranates are in bloom," and she adds, "Which I have laid up for you, my beloved" (v. 13).

It is the same season of budding vines, but the one who was once invited is now the first to invite.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

If the person you love also loves you deeply, what is the best part of yourself you would want to save for them?

The beloved describes her beauty piece by piece (vv. 1-9), and she answers, "I am my beloved's, And his desire is toward me." Then she is the one who invites him. The "pleasant fruits, new and old" at their gates are the same words 4:13 and 4:16 use for her own self; she has had them ready all along, and she tells him, "Which I have laid up for you, my beloved" (vv. 10-13).
