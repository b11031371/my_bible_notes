## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 4:11-18

**11** For we who live are always delivered to death for Jesus' sake, that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our mortal flesh.

**12** So then death is working in us, but life in you.

**13** And since we have the same spirit of faith, according to what is written, "I believed and therefore I spoke," we also believe and therefore speak,

**14** knowing that He who raised up the Lord Jesus will also raise us up with Jesus, and will present us with you.

**15** For all things are for your sakes, that grace, having spread through the many, may cause thanksgiving to abound to the glory of God.

**16** Therefore we do not lose heart. Even though our outward man is perishing, yet the inward man is being renewed day by day.

**17** For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, is working for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory,

**18** while we do not look at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen are temporary, but the things which are not seen are eternal.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul says that they who live are always delivered to death for Jesus' sake, that the life of Jesus may be manifested in their mortal flesh; death is working in them, but life in the Corinthians (vv. 11–12).
- Paul says that, according to what is written, "I believed and therefore I spoke," they also believe and therefore speak, knowing that He who raised up the Lord Jesus will also raise them up with Jesus; all things are for the Corinthians' sakes, that thanksgiving may abound to the glory of God (vv. 13–15).
- Paul says that therefore they do not lose heart; though the outward man is perishing, the inward man is being renewed day by day, and their light, momentary affliction is working a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory, as they look at the things which are not seen (vv. 16–18).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "Therefore we do not lose heart"

> Therefore is part of the answer, because it points us back to what Paul just wrote. Paul just explained that his death-like trials made for more effective, life giving ministry for the Corinthian Christians. Knowing this made him not lose heart in the midst of trials and suffering. [1]

The other reason lies in the second half of verse 16: the decay of the outward man is not wasted; afflictions outwardly become gain inwardly, and even the best of people need their inward man renewed day by day [2].

So Paul's not losing heart did not depend on his circumstances improving: he knew his suffering was bringing life to others and renewal within himself.

---

### "Light affliction … a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory"

> They did as it were put the heavenly glory in one scale and their earthly sufferings in the other … they found afflictions to be light, and the glory of heaven to be a far more exceeding weight. [2]

Weighed this way, suffering for Christ is "worth it" [3]. The key is verse 18: looking at the things which are not seen, because they are eternal.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

When you run into difficulties, what do you usually fix your eyes on? The pain the difficulty brings, or a greater goal beyond it?

Paul fixed his eyes on the things which are not seen, because "the things which are seen are temporary, but the things which are not seen are eternal" (v. 18). So in his eyes, the affliction was "light" and "but for a moment," while the glory was "a far more exceeding and eternal weight" (v. 17).
