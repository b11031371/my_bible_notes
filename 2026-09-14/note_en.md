## II. Key Summary

- Chapter 4 fills in the family lines of Judah and Simeon, and pauses on Jabez: his mother named him Jabez "because I bore him in pain," and Jabez called on the God of Israel to bless him, enlarge his territory, keep His hand with him, and keep him from evil — "so God granted him what he requested" (4:9–10).
- Chapter 5 records the tribes east of the Jordan — Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh. Reuben lost his birthright because he defiled his father's bed; in their war with the Hagrites "they cried out to God in the battle" and "He heeded their prayer, because they put their trust in Him" (5:20); later they were unfaithful to the God of their fathers, followed other gods, and were carried away by the king of Assyria "to this day" (5:25–26).
- Chapter 6 records the tribe of Levi: the line of high priests from Aaron down to Jehozadak, who went into captivity; the singers Heman, Asaph, and Ethan, whom David appointed over the service of song after the ark came to rest; Aaron's sons offering sacrifices and making atonement for Israel; and the cities and common-lands given to the Levites from each tribe.

## III. Passage Analysis

### His name meant "pain," but he didn't just accept it

> "That I may not cause pain" sounds like the word "pain" in the original — a wordplay showing that Jabez's prayer was not about being afraid of suffering, but about not wanting the "pain" his mother spoke of to become his destiny, and not wanting the name "Jabez" to become a curse. [1]

Jabez was labeled "pain" from the day he was born, and every time someone called his name, it reminded him. Instead of just fighting it on his own, he brought it to God and asked — and God really did answer him.

---

### Skilled fighters who still cried out to God

> Though they had a powerful army, they relied not on that, but on the divine power... In our spiritual conflicts, we must look up to heaven for strength; and it is the believing prayer that will be the prevailing prayer. [2]

These 44,000-plus men could handle shields and bows, so they had every reason to feel confident — yet they still cried out to God in the middle of the battle. The passage says God answered them "because they put their trust in Him" (5:20), not because they were strong enough.
