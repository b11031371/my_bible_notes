## II. Key Summary

- 9:35–44 lists the family line of Saul, from Jeiel, Kish, and Saul down to Jonathan's descendant Azel and his six sons.
- The Philistines defeated Israel on Mount Gilboa. Saul's three sons were killed, and the badly wounded Saul fell on his own sword; the Israelites abandoned their cities, the Philistines fastened Saul's head in the temple of Dagon, and the valiant men of Jabesh Gilead brought back the bodies, buried them, and fasted seven days.
- The passage gives the reason for Saul's death: he was unfaithful to the LORD, did not keep the word of the LORD, and consulted a medium instead of inquiring of the LORD, so the LORD killed him and turned the kingdom over to David the son of Jesse.

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### He Asked, But It Didn't Count

> "While Saul was inquiring of God, he was also 'not keeping the word of the LORD.' That shows he only wanted to know his future, not to do God's will — so in God's eyes, it was the same as 'not inquiring of the LORD.'" [1]

Saul did say something to God, but he only wanted to know what was coming next, not what God wanted him to do. To God, really asking means being ready to listen and obey.

---

### The King Fell, but God's Plan Didn't Stop

> "God always has His valiant men. When one servant passes the scene, another arises to take his place. If Saul is gone, God raises up a David… God's work is bigger than any man, or any group of people." [2]

Israel's king was dead and the army had scattered, so it looked like everything was over. But the last line says God "turned the kingdom over to David" — where people failed, God kept going.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Last time you hit a problem, who did you ask first?

Saul ran to a medium instead of truly inquiring of the LORD; God is looking for people who ask Him and then follow His word.
