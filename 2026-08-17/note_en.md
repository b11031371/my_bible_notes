## II. Key Summary

- The guide brought Ezekiel into the sanctuary and measured it—forty cubits long and twenty cubits wide; then he went inside alone, measured a room twenty cubits square, and told Ezekiel, "This is the Most Holy Place" (vv. 1–4).
- Three stories of side chambers were built all around the temple, thirty chambers in each story, growing wider the higher they went; their beams rested on ledges in the wall rather than being fastened into the temple wall itself (vv. 5–11).
- Inside, every wall was paneled with wood from the floor to above the door, and cherubim and palm trees were carved on the walls and on the doors, a palm tree between every two cherubim; a wooden altar also stood there, and the guide said to Ezekiel, "This is the table that is before the Lord" (vv. 16–22).

## III. Passage Analysis

### The Farther In You Go, the Narrower the Door

> The temple Ezekiel saw, like Solomon's temple, was divided into three parts: the vestibule, the holy place, and the Most Holy Place. Note especially that the entrances to the vestibule, the holy place, and the Most Holy Place were fourteen cubits, ten cubits, and six cubits wide. The entrances grow progressively smaller, symbolizing that the holiness of the temple keeps rising. [1]

This chapter just keeps reporting numbers, but there's a pattern hidden inside them: the closer you get to where God is, the smaller the doorway. God isn't shutting people out—He's letting them know that going farther in is something you have to take seriously.

---

### There's a Table at the Very Center

> It is called a table, as being that at which the Lord will take delight in His people, as at a feast. [2]

After passing through doors that got narrower one by one, what Ezekiel saw at the very center was "the table that is before the LORD" (some commentators link its meaning to the showbread table in the holy place; see Exodus 25:30; Leviticus 24:5–9 [1]). God draws the lines that clearly and that strictly, and at the very center He sets a table. A table is for a feast—that is where the Lord takes delight in His people.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Do you think the boundaries God gives you are there to keep you out, or to bring you in?

Ezekiel kept walking inward, and every door was narrower than the last—but what waited for him at the very center was a table the Lord Himself had set.
