## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 1:1-7

**1** Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, To the church of God which is at Corinth, with all the saints who are in all Achaia:

**2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort,

**4** who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble, with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.

**5** For as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also abounds through Christ.

**6** Now if we are afflicted, it is for your consolation and salvation, which is effective for enduring the same sufferings which we also suffer. Or if we are comforted, it is for your consolation and salvation.

**7** And our hope for you is steadfast, because we know that as you are partakers of the sufferings, so also you will partake of the consolation.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, writes with Timothy to the church of God at Corinth and all the saints in Achaia, wishing them grace and peace (vv. 1–2).
- Paul praises "the Father of mercies and God of all comfort," who comforts them in all their tribulation so that they can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort they received from God (vv. 3–4).
- Paul says that as the sufferings of Christ abound in them, their consolation abounds through Christ; whether afflicted or comforted, it is for the Corinthians' consolation and salvation; and his hope for them is steadfast, because as they share in the sufferings, they will share in the consolation (vv. 5–7).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "The God of All Comfort"

> In the New Testament, the meaning of comfort is not only gentle sympathy. Its root meaning is the Latin word fortis. Fortis means courage. Christian comfort is a comfort that brings courage to a person, enabling him to deal with the problems of life. [1]

In verses 3–7 the words "comfort" and "consolation" keep coming back, and verse 6 says this consolation "is effective for enduring the same sufferings which we also suffer."

The comfort God gives is strength to keep going.

---

### "The Same Sufferings"

> Significantly, Paul writes of the same sufferings. It is unlikely the Corinthian Christians were suffering in exactly the same way Paul was. Probably, not one of them could match the list Paul made in 2 Corinthians 11:23-28. Yet, Paul can say they are the same sufferings because he recognizes that the exact circumstances of suffering are not as important as what God does and wants to do through the suffering. [2]

In verse 6 Paul speaks of "enduring the same sufferings which we also suffer," and in verse 7 he says, "as you are partakers of the sufferings, so also you will partake of the consolation."

The sufferings don't have to look identical for the consolation to be shared.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Think about the hardest stretch you have been through—what got you through it? If someone around you is going through a similar trouble, how would you pass that comfort on?

God comforts us in all our tribulation, "that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble, with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God," a comfort that helps us endure (vv. 4, 6).
