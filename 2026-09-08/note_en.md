## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** Psalm 119:105-120

**105** Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.

**106** I have sworn and confirmed that I will keep Your righteous judgments.

**107** I am afflicted very much; revive me, O LORD, according to Your word.

**120** My flesh trembles for fear of You, and I am afraid of Your judgments.

---

## II. Key Summary

- The psalmist says God's word is a lamp to his feet and a light to his path; he has sworn to keep God's righteous judgments, and in his affliction he asks God to revive him according to His word (vv. 105-108).
- He says his life is continually in danger and the wicked have laid a snare for him, yet he has not forgotten God's law or strayed from His precepts, and he has taken God's testimonies as a heritage forever (vv. 109-112).
- He says he hates the double-minded and loves God's law, calls God his hiding place and shield, and twice asks God to hold him up; he also says God puts away the wicked like dross, and that his flesh trembles for fear of God (vv. 113-120).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### A lamp that reaches your feet, not just your eyes

> It must be not only a light to our eyes, to gratify them, and fill our heads with speculations, but a light to our feet and to our path, to direct us in the right ordering of our conversation, both in the choice of our way in general and in the particular steps we take in that way. [1]

In verse 105 the lamp and the light land on "my feet" and "my path." If you understand a verse and agree with it but never walk it out, the lamp hasn't actually reached your feet yet.

---

### When your heart is split in two

> Double-minded people are people who know about God but are not fully determined to worship and serve him only. They want both God and the world. They want the benefits of true religion, but they want their sin too. The Psalmist hates this double-mindedness; he also hates it in himself. [2]

The Hebrew word for "double-minded" in verse 113 appears only here in the Bible, and it pictures a heart divided down the middle. The psalmist doesn't grit his teeth and hold himself steady — he turns around and asks God twice, "Uphold me," "Hold me up" (vv. 116-117).

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Is there something in your life right now where you know what God's word says, but you haven't actually decided to do it?

The psalmist didn't hold himself up either; he prayed, "Hold me up, and I shall be safe" (v. 117).
