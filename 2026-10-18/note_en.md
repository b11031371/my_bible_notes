## II. Key Summary

- Verses 25–31 list twelve men, each over one area: the king's treasuries, the storehouses, the work of the field, the vineyards, the produce for wine, the olive and sycamore trees, the store of oil, the herds in Sharon and in the valleys, the camels, the donkeys, and the flocks.
- The passage records where several of them came from, such as Shimei the Ramathite, Obil the Ishmaelite, and Jaziz the Hagrite; verse 31 sums up: "All these were the officials over King David's property."
- Verses 32–34 list the people around the king: his uncle Jehonathan as counselor and scribe, Jehiel with the king's sons, Ahithophel as counselor, Hushai as the king's companion, Jehoiada and Abiathar after Ahithophel, and Joab as general of the army.

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### Each Man Placed Where He Fit Best

> "It is worthy of remark, that Obil, an Ishmaelite or Arab, was put over the camels which is a creature of Arabia; and that Jaziz, a Hagarene, (the Hagarenes were shepherds by profession,) was put over the flocks: nothing went by favour; each was appointed to the office for which he was best qualified; and thus men of worth were encouraged, and the public service effectually promoted." [1]

Verse 30 notes that Obil was an "Ishmaelite," and verse 31 that Jaziz was a "Hagrite." From these backgrounds, we can see that David assigned work by each person's strengths.

Each man was placed where he was best qualified.

---

### Farming and Herding Also Served the Temple

> "Whether offering sacrifices and singing in the temple, or tilling and herding in the fields; whether guarding the temple gates or guarding the borders, every task had the purpose of building the temple, with no division between sacred and secular." [2]

The men over the fields, herds, and flocks in verses 25–31 are listed among David's arrangements, together with the temple service in chapters 23–26. The next chapter also mentions that the officials over the king's property were among those David gathered for building the temple (28:1).

Farming, herding, and service in the temple all aimed at the same goal: building the temple.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

What is one thing someone else has put in your charge right now, and how have you taken care of it?

Just as Paul, teaching the church in Ephesus, called Jesus Christ the chief cornerstone, in whom "the whole building, being fitted together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord" (Eph. 2:20-21), the treasuries, fields, vineyards, and herds in these twelve men's hands were all entrusted to them by the king (v. 31). Both are called a temple: the physical temple David wanted to build was, by God's will, entrusted to Solomon (1 Chr. 22:6-10), while believers are built into a temple through Jesus; whatever work or service they are entrusted with, Christ should be the one in charge [2].
