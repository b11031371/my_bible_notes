## II. Key Summary

- After the fall of Jerusalem, God tells Ezekiel to wail over Egypt: Egypt and Pharaoh are cast down to the depths of the earth, laid among those who go down to the Pit.
- The passage names one fallen power after another already down there — Assyria, Elam, Meshech, Tubal, Edom, Sidon, and the princes of the north — repeating that they "caused terror in the land of the living" and now "bear their shame with those who go down to the Pit."
- At the end, Pharaoh sees them and is "comforted" over his own slain army; the whole passage closes with God saying, "For I have caused My terror in the land of the living."

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### The Terror You Cause Comes Back as Shame

> Because of all the evil these great powers did while they lived — "who caused terror in the land of the living" — they now share one and the same fate: cast down to Sheol, remembered by no one, "bearing their shame with those who go down to the Pit." Egypt will not escape it either. [1]

Assyria, Elam, and Edom all ran on the same strategy: make everyone afraid of you. The passage names them one by one with the same line, showing that the standing they bought with fear leaves them nothing in the Pit but shame.

---

### "Everyone Else Fell Too" Is No Real Comfort

> with the melancholy satisfaction of not being alone, but of having other kingdoms companions in his downfall. This shall be his only comfort—a very poor one! [2]

Pharaoh's one consolation is discovering that others ended up just as badly. The passage uses that picture to make a point: "everybody does it" may sound like relief, but nothing has actually changed.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

When you have some influence — friends listen to you, people care what you think — what do you usually do with it?

The powers in this passage once made everyone afraid, and all they carry in the end is shame; the question God asks is what you did with the power you had.
