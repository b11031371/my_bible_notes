## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 3:12-18

**12** Therefore, since we have such hope, we use great boldness of speech—

**13** unlike Moses, who put a veil over his face so that the children of Israel could not look steadily at the end of what was passing away.

**14** But their minds were blinded. For until this day the same veil remains unlifted in the reading of the Old Testament, because the veil is taken away in Christ.

**15** But even to this day, when Moses is read, a veil lies on their heart.

**16** Nevertheless when one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away.

**17** Now the Lord is the Spirit; and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.

**18** But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul says that since they have such hope, they use great boldness of speech, unlike Moses, who put a veil over his face so that Israel could not look steadily at the end of what was passing away (vv. 12–13).
- Paul says Israel's minds were blinded; to this day, when the Old Testament and Moses are read, a veil lies on their heart. The veil is taken away in Christ, and when one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away (vv. 14–16).
- Paul says the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty; we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory (vv. 17–18).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "The veil is taken away in Christ"

> The veil in these verses has two meanings. One refers to Israel's prejudice against Christ, or the prejudice that comes from valuing the law, which … kept them from knowing salvation. The other refers to the barrier between God and man, and that barrier is the law. [1]

The veil moves from Moses' face (v. 13) to Israel's heart (v. 15): when they read Moses, they still think Moses' ministry is more glorious than that of Jesus [2]. Verse 14 says the veil "is taken away in Christ," meaning the barrier of the law has been removed; verses 15–16 say a veil still lies on their heart and is taken away "when one turns to the Lord," meaning the prejudice in the heart [1].

So the barrier between God and people has already been removed in Christ; the veil that remains lies on the heart, and it is lifted when the heart turns to the Lord.

---

### "With unveiled face … being transformed into the same image"

> As we behold the glory of God, we will be transformed. God will change our lives and change us from the inside out. Though the old covenant had its glory, it could never transform lives through the law. [2]

The previous passage (3:1-11) said the letter engraved on stone cannot change the heart, and so "the letter kills"; verse 18 says that as people come before the Lord with unveiled face and behold His glory, their life is gradually changed into the Lord\'s image [1].

This is how "the Spirit gives life": the one who changes people into the Lord\'s image is the Spirit of the Lord.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

If you sensed that a Christian was different from other people, what do you think that difference would be?

Paul says that we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, "are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord" (3:18); a life like this, drawn from fellowship with the Lord, is what 2:15 calls "the fragrance of Christ" [3].
