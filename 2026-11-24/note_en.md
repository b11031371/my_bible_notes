## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 2:12-17

**12** Furthermore, when I came to Troas to preach Christ's gospel, and a door was opened to me by the Lord,

**13** I had no rest in my spirit, because I did not find Titus my brother; but taking my leave of them, I departed for Macedonia.

**14** Now thanks be to God who always leads us in triumph in Christ, and through us diffuses the fragrance of His knowledge in every place.

**15** For we are to God the fragrance of Christ among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing.

**16** To the one we are the aroma of death leading to death, and to the other the aroma of life leading to life. And who is sufficient for these things?

**17** For we are not, as so many, peddling the word of God; but as of sincerity, but as from God, we speak in the sight of God in Christ.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul came to Troas to preach Christ's gospel, and a door was opened to him by the Lord. But he had no rest in his spirit because he did not find Titus his brother, so he took his leave of them and departed for Macedonia (vv. 12–13).
- Paul thanks God who always leads them in triumph in Christ and through them diffuses the fragrance of His knowledge in every place. They are to God the fragrance of Christ among those being saved and among those perishing: to the one the aroma of death leading to death, to the other the aroma of life leading to life (vv. 14–16).
- Paul asks, "Who is sufficient for these things?" He says they are not, as so many, peddling the word of God, but speak as of sincerity, as from God, in the sight of God in Christ (vv. 16–17).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### From "no rest in my spirit" to "thanks be to God"

> Paul's defense of his travels breaks off at verse 13 and only resumes at 7:5. The long section inserted in between, 2:14 to 7:4, should not be seen as the content of another letter of Paul's, but as words of thanksgiving he wrote out spontaneously in his joy. [1]

Before setting out, Paul had Titus carry the letter he had written with tears to Corinth, and they arranged to meet in Troas. When Titus did not come, Paul was anxious to know how the Corinthian church had responded to that letter; this is the "no rest in my spirit" of verse 13 [1]. Later he met Titus in Macedonia and heard that the church's crisis had passed (7:5-8) [1].

So in verse 13 Paul is still talking about having no rest, and in verse 14 he bursts out, "thanks be to God": by the time he wrote this letter, he already knew how things had turned out.

---

### "We speak in the sight of God in Christ"

> This verse can serve as the conclusion of the first section on Paul's travels (chapters 1 and 2). Paul said so much in order to make the believers understand that his footsteps were not like those of the false teachers, who for gain confused the word of God, or for gain changed their travels, but that he sincerely decided his steps according to God's leading. [1]

Paul's several changes of plan (2:1, 2:4, 2:13) all answer those who said he was saying "Yes, Yes" and "No, No" (1:17) [1]. The fragrance in verses 14–16 belongs on this same line: faithful ministers are accepted by God whatever their success, and among those being saved and those perishing alike, they are to God a sweet savour of Christ [2].

Both verse 15 and verse 17 say "to God" / "in the sight of God": his plans could change, but before God Paul always gave off the same fragrance of Christ.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

If you kept doing something for others, but it wasn't always accepted, and you were even questioned like Paul was, what would keep you going?

Paul says, "we are to God the fragrance of Christ among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing" (v. 15); so he went on speaking "as of sincerity, but as from God, … in the sight of God in Christ" (v. 17).
