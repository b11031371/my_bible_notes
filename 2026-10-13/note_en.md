## II. Key Summary

- Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu died before their father and had no children, so Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests; David, together with Zadok and Ahimelech, divided them according to the schedule of their service.
- Eleazar's family had sixteen heads and Ithamar's had eight; all of them were ordered by lot into twenty-four divisions, and the scribe Shemaiah wrote down their names before the king, the leaders, and the priests.
- The rest of the Levites also cast lots before King David just as the sons of Aaron did, and the text notes that "the chief fathers did just as their younger brethren."

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### Drawing Lots: No One Could Cry "Unfair"

> "It was made by lot, that the disposal thereof might be of the Lord, and so all quarrels and contentions might be prevented, and no man could be charged with partiality, nor could any say that they had wrong done them." [1]

David was the king and could have simply picked who went first, but he handed the order over to God. That way nobody could complain about favoritism, and nobody had to fight for a better spot.

---

### Older and Younger on the Same Line

> "'The chief fathers did just as their younger brethren' shows that everyone was treated the same when the lots were cast, with no distinction made for seniority, ability, or experience... no one is more important than anyone else, and no one is more qualified than anyone else." [2]

In this draw, being older or coming from a bigger family didn't move you up the line. Getting to serve in God's house wasn't about who had the most seniority or status; it was a chance God gave.

## IV. Reflection Question

The last time your class, team, or family used a draw or a rotation to hand out jobs, where did you end up, and what was your first reaction?

In 1 Chronicles 24, the chief fathers cast lots just as their younger brethren did, so before God no one is more qualified than anyone else, and every place on the schedule is a chance to serve that God Himself hands out.
