## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** Song of Solomon 1:1-17

**1** The song of songs, which is Solomon’s.

## II. Key Summary

- The woman says his love is better than wine, his name is ointment poured forth, and therefore the virgins love him; she says "Draw me away! We will run after you," and the king has brought her into his chambers (vv. 2-4).
- She tells the daughters of Jerusalem, "I am dark, but lovely," and asks them not to look upon her because the sun has tanned her; her mother's sons were angry with her and made her the keeper of the vineyards, but her own vineyard she has not kept (vv. 5-6).
- He compares her to his filly among Pharaoh's chariots, she calls him a bundle of myrrh and a cluster of henna blooms; he says "Behold, you are fair, my love!" and she answers "Behold, you are handsome, my beloved!" (vv. 9-16).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "I Am Dark, but Lovely"

> "The self-doubt the maiden had regarding her own appearance should not be overstated. She did feel, in some ways, unattractive and unworthy (Do not look upon me, because I am dark). Yet at the same time she could say she is lovely." [1]

In verse 5 she says two things at once: "dark" and "lovely." In verse 6 she asks not to be looked upon because the sun has tanned her. Then in verses 8 and 15 he calls her "fairest among women" and says "you are fair" twice.

She calls herself dark, and he calls her fair.

---

### "Draw Me Away"

> "Draw me away: This was the logical desire of a woman so taken with loving desire towards her beloved. She wanted to be with him, and to be one with him." [1]

At the start of the chapter she says, "Draw me away! We will run after you." In the middle she asks him where he feeds his flock and where he makes it rest at noon. At the end she says, "Our bed is green. The beams of our houses are cedar, and our rafters of fir." At the start she asks him to draw her; by the end she is already speaking of "our" bed and "our" houses.

Again and again in this chapter, she voices the same longing: to be with him.

## IV. Reflection Question

If there were a love you really wanted to pursue, would you care more about what people around you say and how they look at you, or about how you look in "that person's" eyes?

She tells the daughters of Jerusalem, "I am dark, but lovely," and asks them not to look upon her because the sun has tanned her; at the same time she asks the one she loves, "Where you feed your flock, where you make it rest at noon." And he answers her, "O fairest among women" (vv. 5-8).
