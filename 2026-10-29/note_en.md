## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** Song of Solomon 6:1-13

**1** Where has your beloved gone, O fairest among women? Where has your beloved turned aside, That we may seek him with you?

**2** My beloved has gone to his garden, To the beds of spices, To feed his flock in the gardens, And to gather lilies.

**3** I am my beloved’s, And my beloved is mine. He feeds his flock among the lilies.

**4** O my love, you are as beautiful as Tirzah, Lovely as Jerusalem, Awesome as an army with banners!

**5** Turn your eyes away from me, For they have overcome me. Your hair is like a flock of goats Going down from Gilead.

**6** Your teeth are like a flock of sheep Which have come up from the washing; Every one bears twins, And none is barren among them.

**7** Like a piece of pomegranate Are your temples behind your veil.

**8** There are sixty queens And eighty concubines, And virgins without number.

**9** My dove, my perfect one, Is the only one, The only one of her mother, The favorite of the one who bore her. The daughters saw her And called her blessed, The queens and the concubines, And they praised her.

**10** Who is she who looks forth as the morning, Fair as the moon, Clear as the sun, Awesome as an army with banners?

**11** I went down to the garden of nuts To see the verdure of the valley, To see whether the vine had budded And the pomegranates had bloomed.

**12** Before I was even aware, My soul had made me As the chariots of my noble people.

**13** Return, return, O Shulamite; Return, return, that we may look upon you! What would you see in the Shulamite— As it were, the dance of the two camps?

## II. Key Summary

- The daughters of Jerusalem ask, "Where has your beloved gone?" and offer to seek him with her; she answers that he has gone to his garden to feed his flock and gather lilies, and says, "I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine." (vv. 1–3)
- The beloved says she is as beautiful as Tirzah, lovely as Jerusalem, awesome as an army with banners; he asks her to turn her eyes away, and compares her hair, teeth, and temples to a flock of goats, a flock of sheep, and a piece of pomegranate. (vv. 4–7)
- He says there are sixty queens, eighty concubines, and virgins without number, but his dove, his perfect one, is the only one; the daughters call her blessed and the queens and concubines praise her. Then come the words "I went down to the garden of nuts" to see the vine and the pomegranates, and finally the call, "Return, return, O Shulamite." (vv. 8–13)

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### Both of Them Return to Their First Words

> The bride once again declares her first love (2:16). … The bridegroom also declares his first love (4:1–9). [1]

In 6:3 she says "I am my beloved's," the words of 2:16 in reverse order. In 6:5–7 he praises her with the goats, the sheep, and the pomegranate, the same pictures he used in 4:1–3. They were apart in 5:6, and in this chapter, when each of them speaks, they say the words from the beginning.

After being apart, both of them come back to their first love.

---

### In Chapter 5 She Praises Him; in Chapter 6 He Praises Her

> As she exalted Him above all others (So 5:10), so He now her. [2]

In 5:9 the daughters ask what makes her beloved "more than another beloved," and she answers that he is "chief among ten thousand," describing him from head to foot (5:10–16). In 6:8–9 it is his turn: there are queens, concubines, and virgins without number, but she "is the only one," and the daughters and the queens call her blessed and praise her.

In chapter 5 she says he stands above ten thousand; in chapter 6 he says she is the only one.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

What makes the person you like or have feelings for most better than anyone else? And if it were their turn, what do you think they would say is good about you?

When the daughters asked the bride, "What is your beloved more than another beloved?" she said he was "chief among ten thousand" (5:9–10). In this chapter it is his turn to lift her up: there are queens and concubines without number, but "my dove, my perfect one, is the only one" (6:8–9).
