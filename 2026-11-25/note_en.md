## I. Today's Reading

**NKJV:** 2 Corinthians 3:1-11

**1** Do we begin again to commend ourselves? Or do we need, as some others, epistles of commendation to you or letters of commendation from you?

**2** You are our epistle written in our hearts, known and read by all men;

**3** clearly you are an epistle of Christ, ministered by us, written not with ink but by the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of flesh, that is, of the heart.

**4** And we have such trust through Christ toward God.

**5** Not that we are sufficient of ourselves to think of anything as being from ourselves, but our sufficiency is from God,

**6** who also made us sufficient as ministers of the new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit; for the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.

**7** But if the ministry of death, written and engraved on stones, was glorious, so that the children of Israel could not look steadily at the face of Moses because of the glory of his countenance, which glory was passing away,

**8** how will the ministry of the Spirit not be more glorious?

**9** For if the ministry of condemnation had glory, the ministry of righteousness exceeds much more in glory.

**10** For even what was made glorious had no glory in this respect, because of the glory that excels.

**11** For if what is passing away was glorious, what remains is much more glorious.

---

## II. Key Summary

- Paul asks whether they are beginning again to commend themselves, or need letters of commendation as some others do. He says the Corinthians are their epistle, an epistle of Christ ministered by them, written not with ink on tablets of stone but by the Spirit of the living God on tablets of flesh, of the heart (vv. 1–3).
- Paul says they have such trust through Christ toward God; they are not sufficient of themselves, but their sufficiency is from God, who made them sufficient as ministers of the new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit, for the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life (vv. 4–6).
- Paul says that if the ministry of death engraved on stones was glorious, so that Israel could not look steadily at Moses' face because of its glory, which was passing away, then the ministry of the Spirit, of righteousness, and what remains is much more glorious (vv. 7–11).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### "As some others, epistles of commendation"

> He points out that there were so many false apostles and false teachers that churches everywhere needed people sent from other churches to carry letters of recommendation proving the bearer's identity. Paul says his shepherding work itself is the proof of his apostolic authority; the Corinthian church, which he founded, can serve as living proof of his qualification as an apostle. [1]

Those who came with letters may have come from Judaizers who held that salvation depended on keeping the law (Acts 15:5), and they slandered Paul for having no letter [1]; when Paul goes on to compare the old and new covenants, he is answering these people who greatly magnified the law of Moses [2].

So verse 3 moves from "epistles" all the way to "tablets of stone" and "tablets of the heart": the comparison in this chapter grows out of this dispute.

---

### "The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life"

> The letter is the law in its outward sense, written on tablets of stone. … It was good in itself, but it gave us no power to serve God, and it did not change our heart; it simply told us what to do. [3]

The "tablets of stone" and "tablets of flesh" in verse 3 echo the prophets' promise: God would write His law "on their hearts" (Jer. 31:33), and take away the heart of stone and give a heart of flesh (Ezek. 36:26) [3]. No one can fully keep the law's demands, because the letter gives no power to do them; it can only sentence the one who breaks it to death [1].

That is why Paul is a "minister of the new covenant" (v. 6): the Spirit of God writes the letter on the tablets of the heart, and this is how "the Spirit gives life."

---

## IV. Reflection Question

You can keep a rule just because you are afraid of being punished, or because your heart really wants to. If a rule only tells you what to do but your heart does not change, how much of it do you think you could keep?

Paul says the epistle of Christ is written "by the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of flesh, that is, of the heart" (v. 3); so God made them "ministers of the new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit; for the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life" (v. 6).
