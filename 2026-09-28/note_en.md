## I. Today's Reading

---

## II. Key Summary

- On the day the ark was set in its place, David first delivered a psalm of thanks into the hand of Asaph and his brethren to thank the LORD (v. 7).
- The psalm calls Israel to give thanks, call on, sing to, talk about, and seek the LORD, and to remember His marvelous works and the judgments of His mouth (vv. 8–14).
- The psalm recalls the covenant God made with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, promising them the land of Canaan; when they were few and wandered as strangers among nations, God let no one harm them and rebuked kings for their sakes (vv. 15–22).

---

## III. Passage Analysis

### Praise Comes in Many Shapes

> In a few verses, David lists a remarkable number of ways (at least eight) one can praise and glorify God. Some of them speak directly to God (such as sing psalms to Him), some speak to others about God's greatness (make known His deeds among the peoples), and some are a conversation with one's self (remember His marvelous works). [1]

Praise isn't only singing on a stage. Telling a friend what God has done, or quietly thinking back on how He brought you through something, counts too.

---

### God Keeps His Promise, Not Based on How Strong We Are

> God's plan of salvation does not depend on the ability, character, or devotion of His chosen people; rather, He "promised with His own mouth and fulfilled it with His own hand." [2]

Israel's ancestors were few, had no land of their own, and kept moving from nation to nation — any king could have crushed them. But God keeps His word. He protected them not because they were impressive, but because He is faithful.

---

## IV. Reflection Question

Think of the last time you "made a deal" with someone — who kept their word, and who didn't?

God never forgot the covenant He made with Abraham, even generations later; when the ancestors were few and wandering, He never once let anyone harm them — God keeps His covenant, and it never depends on how strong we are.
